## Overview

This project focused on building and scaling a vendor performance framework for a large online food delivery marketplace.

The framework combined two complementary mechanisms:

- performance-based rewards for vendors with strong operational behavior;
- performance-related cost logic for vendors exceeding acceptable operational thresholds.

The objective was to create a more structured, measurable, and fair way to manage vendor performance at scale.

Rather than treating performance issues as isolated cases, the project translated operational behavior into a repeatable monthly workflow supported by KPI logic, automated calculations, reporting, vendor communication, and cross-functional governance.

---

## Business Context

Marketplace quality depends heavily on vendor behavior.

When vendors repeatedly cancel orders, create order issues, or delay key operational steps, the impact is not limited to one transaction. It affects customer experience, operational workload, delivery reliability, and platform trust.

At the same time, not every vendor should be treated the same way. A scalable performance system needs to distinguish between:

- vendors with consistently strong performance;
- vendors with occasional operational issues;
- vendors repeatedly exceeding acceptable thresholds;
- new vendors that still need onboarding time;
- vendors requiring training, follow-up, or intervention.

The project was designed to make this treatment more consistent, transparent, and data-driven.

---

## Problem

Before the framework became more structured, several parts of vendor performance management required manual handling or fragmented reporting.

The main challenges included:

- inconsistent visibility into vendor performance eligibility;
- manual monthly operational workflows;
- limited historical tracking of vendor inclusion and performance outcomes;
- difficulty connecting KPI logic with downstream financial, operational, and communication processes;
- the need to balance corrective mechanisms with positive incentives;
- the risk of unfair treatment if performance windows, thresholds, or exceptional cases were not handled carefully.

The goal was to move from ad hoc performance handling toward a repeatable performance governance system.

---

## Solution

The project developed a structured Reward & Cost framework around vendor performance KPIs.

The solution included:

- KPI-based eligibility rules;
- vendor inclusion logic;
- monthly performance calculation workflows;
- reward eligibility analysis;
- operational cost calculation logic;
- reporting outputs for business and operations teams;
- vendor-facing performance visibility;
- communication workflows;
- historical archiving and monitoring;
- cross-functional coordination with operations, finance, product, and business teams.

The framework helped turn vendor performance management into a scalable monthly process.

## Analytical Logic

The framework was based on a set of operational performance indicators.

The exact internal thresholds and business rules are not included in this public version, but the logic followed a general structure:

1. define eligible vendor population;
2. calculate vendor-level performance KPIs for the period;
3. compare each vendor’s performance against acceptable thresholds;
4. identify vendors eligible for rewards;
5. identify vendors exceeding operational limits;
6. generate outputs for reporting, follow-up, and downstream workflows;
7. archive monthly results for historical tracking.

The logic was designed to support both fairness and scalability.

---

## Key Components

### Vendor Eligibility Logic

The framework required a clear definition of which vendors should be included in the program.

This included rules related to vendor lifecycle, operational activity, category, and program scope. The purpose was to make sure the framework applied consistently and did not mix vendors that were not comparable.

### KPI Calculation Layer

The project translated operational events into vendor-level KPIs.

These KPIs were used to evaluate whether a vendor stayed within acceptable performance limits or qualified for positive recognition.

### Cost Calculation Workflow

For vendors exceeding defined operational limits, the framework generated structured monthly outputs for downstream processing.

This required careful validation because the output was connected to sensitive financial and vendor-facing workflows.

### Reward Workflow

For vendors with strong performance, the framework generated reward eligibility outputs.

The reward side was important because the project was not only about corrective action. It also aimed to create positive reinforcement for vendors maintaining strong operational quality.

### Vendor Communication

The project included vendor-facing communication flows to explain performance outcomes, encourage improvement, and increase awareness of the performance framework.

In the public version, internal message templates, communication links, and operational details are intentionally excluded.

### Reporting and Governance

The monthly outputs were shared with relevant internal stakeholders to support monitoring, financial alignment, operational follow-up, and program governance.

## Workflow Design

The project combined manual, semi-automated, and automated steps.

Over time, parts of the workflow were improved through automation and better integration with downstream systems. Other parts still required structured manual execution, validation, or stakeholder coordination.

A major part of the project was identifying which recurring steps could be automated and which required operational review because of business sensitivity.

## Why This Project Was Sensitive

This project was sensitive because it affected vendor-facing outcomes and financial processes.

Any error in eligibility, threshold logic, vendor inclusion, or calculation output could lead to incorrect treatment of vendors. For that reason, the workflow required:

- careful data validation;
- historical tracking;
- stakeholder alignment;
- clear timing of monthly processes;
- documentation of exceptional cases;
- coordination between analytics, operations, finance, and product teams.

This made the project closer to a performance governance system than a simple reporting task.
