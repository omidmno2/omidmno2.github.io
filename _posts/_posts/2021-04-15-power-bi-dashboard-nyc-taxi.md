## Overview

This project focused on building a Power BI dashboard using public NYC Taxi & Limousine Commission trip data.

The goal was to analyze taxi trip behavior, demand distribution, revenue patterns, and operational KPIs through an interactive business intelligence dashboard.

The project was developed as a data visualization and dashboarding exercise, with the objective of transforming raw trip data into a clear analytical view for decision-making.

---

## Business Context

Urban mobility systems generate large volumes of trip data.

Taxi trip records can help answer important operational and business questions, such as:

- when demand is highest;
- which locations generate the most trips;
- how trip distance and duration vary;
- how revenue changes across time periods;
- which payment types are most common;
- how passenger behavior differs across zones or hours.

A dashboard can help stakeholders monitor these patterns without needing to inspect raw datasets manually.

---

## Problem

Raw transportation datasets are often too large and detailed for direct interpretation.

The main challenge was to convert trip-level records into a dashboard that could quickly answer practical questions about demand, revenue, trip behavior, and service patterns.

The project needed to address:

- data cleaning and preparation;
- KPI definition;
- aggregation of trip-level records;
- time-based analysis;
- geographic or zone-based comparison;
- clear visual design;
- interactive filtering;
- business-friendly interpretation.

---

## Objective

The objective was to create an interactive Power BI dashboard that could support analysis of NYC taxi operations.

The dashboard aimed to answer questions such as:

- How many trips were completed in each period?
- How does demand vary by hour, day, or month?
- What are the main revenue patterns?
- What is the average trip distance and duration?
- Which zones or locations show stronger demand?
- How do payment methods differ across trips?
- What patterns can be useful for operational planning?

---

## Data Preparation

The project used public NYC taxi trip data.

Before dashboard creation, the data required preparation and transformation.

The preparation process included:

- importing trip records;
- reviewing column types;
- handling missing or inconsistent values;
- creating calculated fields;
- extracting time-based features;
- aggregating trip-level data into dashboard-ready measures;
- preparing KPIs for visualization.

The goal was to make the dataset suitable for interactive analysis in Power BI.

---

## KPI Design

The dashboard was structured around several core mobility and business KPIs.

These included:

- total trips;
- total fare or revenue;
- average trip distance;
- average trip duration;
- average fare per trip;
- passenger count;
- payment type distribution;
- demand by time period;
- trip distribution by pickup or drop-off area.

The KPIs were selected to give both operational and business visibility.

---

## Dashboard Design

The dashboard was designed to be exploratory and easy to read.

It included visual components such as:

- KPI cards for high-level metrics;
- line charts for time trends;
- bar charts for category comparison;
- maps or location-based visuals;
- filters for time, location, payment type, or trip attributes;
- summary visuals for demand and revenue behavior.

The dashboard allowed users to move from a high-level overview to more detailed analysis through filters and interactions.

---

## Analysis Areas

### Demand Pattern Analysis

The dashboard helped identify when trip demand was higher or lower.

This type of analysis can support decisions around fleet availability, driver allocation, and peak-hour planning.

### Revenue Analysis

Revenue-related metrics helped show how fare patterns changed across time and trip characteristics.

This can support business monitoring and performance comparison.

### Trip Behavior Analysis

Trip distance, duration, and passenger count were used to understand how customers used taxi services.

This helped distinguish short urban rides from longer trips.

### Location-Based Analysis

Pickup and drop-off patterns were used to understand spatial demand distribution.

This can help identify high-demand zones and areas requiring more operational attention.

## Business Interpretation

The dashboard showed how transportation data can support operational decision-making.

Instead of relying on raw trip logs, users could quickly understand:

- where and when demand is concentrated;
- how revenue changes over time;
- which trip characteristics drive performance;
- whether certain locations or time windows require more attention;
- how customer behavior varies across the city.

This type of dashboard can support mobility planning, operational monitoring, and business intelligence.

---

## Limitations

This project was a dashboarding and analytics exercise rather than a production BI system.

Main limitations included:

- limited integration with live data sources;
- no automated refresh pipeline;
- limited predictive modeling;
- no full cost or profitability model;
- potential data quality limitations in public trip records;
- limited external context such as weather, events, or traffic conditions.

A production version would require automated data pipelines, governance rules, data validation, and stakeholder-specific dashboard views.
