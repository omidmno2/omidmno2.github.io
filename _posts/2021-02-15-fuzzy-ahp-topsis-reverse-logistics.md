## Overview

This project focused on prioritizing solutions for overcoming barriers in reverse logistics.

Reverse logistics refers to the flow of products, materials, packaging, or waste from customers or downstream users back toward producers, recyclers, repair centers, or disposal systems.

The project used a multi-criteria decision-making approach based on **Fuzzy AHP** and **TOPSIS** to evaluate possible solutions under uncertainty.

The goal was to support structured decision-making in a sustainability-related operations management problem.

---

## Business and Operations Context

Reverse logistics is an important part of sustainable supply chain management.

It can support:

- product returns;
- recycling;
- remanufacturing;
- repair and reuse;
- waste reduction;
- circular economy initiatives;
- environmental responsibility.

However, implementing reverse logistics is often difficult because companies face several barriers at the same time.

These barriers may include operational complexity, cost uncertainty, lack of infrastructure, weak coordination, limited technology, unclear regulations, or low customer participation.

Because these challenges are multi-dimensional, decision-makers need a structured method to compare possible solutions.

---

## Problem

The main problem was to prioritize improvement strategies for reverse logistics when several criteria and uncertainties exist.

A simple ranking based on one factor is not enough, because a solution may be strong in one dimension but weak in another.

For example, one solution may be cost-effective but difficult to implement. Another may be environmentally strong but operationally complex.

The project addressed this type of decision problem using multi-criteria decision-making methods.

---

## Objective

The objective was to build a decision-support framework that could:

- identify key barriers to reverse logistics;
- define evaluation criteria;
- compare possible solutions;
- handle uncertainty in expert judgment;
- rank alternatives using a structured methodology;
- support more transparent decision-making.

---

## Methodology

The project combined two methods:

1. **Fuzzy AHP** to determine the relative importance of criteria;
2. **TOPSIS** to rank the proposed solutions based on their distance from ideal and negative-ideal solutions.

This combination is useful when decisions depend on multiple qualitative and quantitative criteria.

---

## Fuzzy AHP

The Analytic Hierarchy Process is commonly used to assign weights to decision criteria through pairwise comparisons.

However, in many real decisions, expert judgments are not perfectly precise. Decision-makers may be more comfortable saying that one criterion is “slightly more important” or “strongly more important” than another, rather than giving an exact numerical value.

For this reason, the project used a fuzzy version of AHP.

Fuzzy AHP helps model uncertainty and ambiguity in human judgment.

In this project, Fuzzy AHP was used to estimate the relative importance of the criteria affecting reverse logistics improvement.

---

## TOPSIS

After weighting the criteria, TOPSIS was used to rank the available solutions.

TOPSIS is based on a simple idea:

> The best alternative should be closest to the ideal solution and farthest from the negative-ideal solution.

The method compares alternatives across weighted criteria and calculates a relative closeness score for each option.

This makes it useful for ranking reverse logistics solutions when several criteria must be considered together.

## Decision Framework

The general decision framework included:

1. defining the decision problem;
2. identifying reverse logistics barriers;
3. defining possible improvement solutions;
4. selecting evaluation criteria;
5. collecting expert judgments;
6. calculating criteria weights using Fuzzy AHP;
7. evaluating alternatives using TOPSIS;
8. ranking the solutions;
9. interpreting the results for decision-making.

## Key Outputs

The project produced:

- a structured decision hierarchy;
- criteria weighting using Fuzzy AHP;
- alternative ranking using TOPSIS;
- comparison of solution priorities;
- interpretation of the most suitable actions for improving reverse logistics.

The main value was not only the final ranking, but the transparent decision-making process behind it.

---

## Business Interpretation

The project showed how operations research methods can support sustainability-related decisions.

Instead of selecting reverse logistics solutions based on intuition, decision-makers can use a structured framework that makes assumptions, criteria, and priorities explicit.

This is especially useful when:

- several stakeholders are involved;
- criteria conflict with each other;
- data is incomplete;
- expert judgment is uncertain;
- the decision has long-term operational and environmental consequences.

---

## Limitations

This was an academic decision-support project.

Main limitations included:

- reliance on expert judgment;
- sensitivity to selected criteria;
- sensitivity to pairwise comparison inputs;
- limited validation with real implementation outcomes;
- no full cost-benefit or operational simulation layer.

The model should be interpreted as a structured decision-support tool, not as an automatic decision-maker.

---

## Future Improvements

The project could be improved by:

- using real company data;
- involving a larger group of experts;
- adding sensitivity analysis;
- comparing results with other MCDM methods;
- integrating cost, risk, and implementation feasibility;
- validating the ranking through real reverse logistics initiatives;
- connecting the decision model to circular economy performance indicators.
