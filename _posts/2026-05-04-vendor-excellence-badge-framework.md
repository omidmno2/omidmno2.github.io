## Overview

This project focused on designing a vendor excellence badge framework for a food delivery marketplace.

The goal was to define a badge that could highlight high-performing vendors in the customer-facing experience while also creating a positive motivation loop for vendors to improve and maintain operational quality.

The project combined marketplace benchmarking, KPI design, eligibility logic, threshold simulation, product thinking, and operational governance.

This public version intentionally excludes internal thresholds, vendor selection rules, product implementation details, design assets, stakeholder names, and confidential decision-making documents.

---

## Business Context

In a marketplace, customers often need quick signals to decide which vendor to trust.

A well-designed badge can help customers identify vendors with stronger quality, reliability, and service experience. At the same time, it can give vendors a visible incentive to improve their performance.

However, badge design is sensitive.

If too many vendors receive the badge, it loses credibility. If too few receive it, it may not create enough motivation or business value. If eligibility is based on weak or unstable rules, the badge can become unfair, easy to manipulate, or misleading for customers.

The project was designed to answer a strategic question:

> How can we define a vendor badge that is meaningful for customers, motivating for vendors, and defensible from a performance-governance perspective?

---

## Problem

Creating a vendor badge is not just a visual or product-design task.

It requires decisions about:

- what “excellent performance” means;
- which KPIs should define eligibility;
- how strict the eligibility thresholds should be;
- how many vendors should receive the badge;
- whether badge recipients are stable over time;
- how to prevent badge quality from deteriorating;
- how to avoid vendor misuse or gaming;
- how to explain the badge to customers and vendors;
- how to monitor badge performance after launch.

Without a structured analytical framework, badge selection can become subjective, unstable, or misaligned with the intended customer experience.

---

## Solution

The solution was a vendor excellence badge framework supported by KPI logic and simulation.

At a high level, the framework included:

- benchmarking badge concepts used by comparable marketplace platforms;
- defining the purpose and positioning of the badge;
- identifying performance dimensions relevant to vendor quality;
- designing eligibility logic based on vendor performance indicators;
- simulating different threshold scenarios;
- analyzing how many vendors would qualify under each scenario;
- reviewing the characteristics of eligible vendors;
- considering product placement and customer-facing communication;
- defining governance considerations for ongoing quality control.

The exact KPI formulas, thresholds, vendor population rules, product specifications, and launch details are not disclosed in this public version.

## Badge Design Principles

The framework was designed around several principles.

### Customer Value

The badge needed to help customers make better choices by highlighting vendors with stronger performance signals.

A badge should not only decorate the interface. It should communicate a meaningful quality signal.

### Vendor Motivation

The badge also needed to create a positive incentive for vendors.

If vendors understand that better performance can lead to greater visibility or recognition, the badge can support behavior improvement.

### Credibility

The badge must remain selective enough to be meaningful.

If too many vendors qualify, customers may stop trusting it. If the logic is too strict or unclear, vendors may not see it as achievable.

### Fairness

Eligibility should be based on consistent and defensible performance logic.

The framework needed to avoid arbitrary selection and support fairer vendor treatment.

### Sustainability

Badge eligibility should not be treated as a one-time decision.

The framework needed ongoing monitoring to ensure vendors with the badge continued to meet quality expectations.

## Analytical Framework

The analytical framework focused on identifying vendors that consistently demonstrated stronger operational and customer-facing performance.

The exact internal KPIs are not disclosed, but the logic considered several high-level dimensions such as:

- operational reliability;
- order quality;
- customer experience;
- performance stability;
- vendor activity and maturity;
- consistency across multiple periods.

The goal was to avoid selecting vendors based on a single short-term snapshot.

A badge should represent sustained quality, not temporary performance.

## Threshold Simulation

A key part of the project was threshold simulation.

A Tableau dashboard was created to help test how different eligibility thresholds would affect badge distribution.

The simulation helped answer questions such as:

- How many vendors qualify under each scenario?
- Which vendors qualify?
- What are the characteristics of selected vendors?
- Does the eligible group look too broad or too narrow?
- How does eligibility change when thresholds become stricter or more flexible?
- Are badge recipients stable across multiple months?
- Does the selected population match the intended meaning of “excellence”?

This helped make badge design more data-driven and less dependent on intuition.

## Governance Considerations

The project included several governance considerations.

### Anti-Gaming

A badge system can create incentives for vendors to optimize only for the visible rules.

The framework therefore considered how to reduce the risk of vendors gaming the system or improving only narrow metrics while weakening overall customer experience.

### Ongoing Quality Control

A vendor that qualifies once should not keep the badge indefinitely without continued performance monitoring.

The framework considered the need for recurring reevaluation and removal logic if performance drops.

### Badge Credibility

The badge should remain meaningful over time.

This requires monitoring the size and quality of the eligible vendor population and adjusting the framework if the badge becomes too broad, too narrow, or misaligned with customer value.

### Operational Feasibility

The framework also considered whether the eligibility process could be maintained manually at first and automated later if the program scaled.
